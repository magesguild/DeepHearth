# Cards and Cardless Play

*Beta research document: preserve the relationship between material modes without
turning either mode into the only correct game.*

The unified physics play constraints in
`09-unified-physics-and-delightful-play.md` apply to both material modes. Cards
and cardless objects are different projections of the same bounded state, not
different realities.

## The Principle

Deep Hearth is playable with cards or without them. Cards change the texture of
play, not the truth of the rules.

The shared constitution remains:

```text
input
-> cost and latency
-> legal operation
-> receiving state
-> output
-> witness
-> Rest or continuation
```

With and without cards, an operation must agree about its purpose, cost, latency,
output shape, scope, failure values, T-Stops, and witness. The materials and host
machinery do not need to be identical.

## Cardless Play

The baseline game can use:

```text
grid
figures
dice
stones
coins
bottle caps
beads
paper
ledgers
```

These materials represent state directly. A stone may be Reserve, a figure may be
a cell, a die may be a clock, and a paper form may be a State Capsule.

Cardless play is not a weaker fallback. It can provide:

- faster entry;
- fewer rules hidden in objects;
- easier improvisation;
- direct attention to bodies, places, and relationships;
- no assumption that capability means ownership;
- access for tables without a particular collection.

Every cardless operation still declares its input, output, cost, latency, scope,
failure values, T-Stop set, and witness.

## Cards as Material Memory

A card externalizes state and possibility. It may carry:

```text
capability
permission
hidden information
resource
organ
land source
creature body
relationship
loot packet
witness
```

A card is not automatically active. It may be:

```text
found
offered
held
inspected
installed
active
dormant
broken
archived
transformed
returned
```

Putting a card in a backpack does not install its capability. The player may
accept, refuse, archive, trade, repair, transform, or return it.

A card may carry a knowledge projection: a map, phrase, job license, archive
fragment, public witness, or contested claim. Its source, scope, custody, and
visibility remain part of the card's state. A card can preserve knowledge without
making the knowledge complete or universally trusted.

At larger organism scales, a card may also represent an aggregate rather than a
single ability: a guild license, neighborhood pressure, ecological population,
route condition, public memory, or community witness. The card should carry only
the state that changes the next legal future. When the table zooms in, that
aggregate may be expanded into local cells; when it zooms out, witnessed local
changes may be compacted back into the card without erasing unresolved effects.

Cards are useful when the table needs:

- visible but bounded state;
- private information;
- prepared capabilities;
- reusable encounter bodies;
- portable session memory;
- provenance and lineage;
- a physical archive of possible futures.

## Host Adapters

Magic: The Gathering is one optional host adapter, not a dependency and not the
source of Deep Hearth's ontology. Magic retains its own cards, mana, zones,
stack, priority, tokens, library, and rules. Deep Hearth receives the declared,
witnessed consequence through an explicit boundary.

Other possible hosts include:

```text
ordinary playing cards
Tarot or oracle cards
tiles
tokens
cord ledgers
digital worlds
```

An adapter preserves host rules and provenance rather than flattening every host
into generic card play.

## Magic and Cardless Equivalence

`KINDLE-RAT` demonstrates the distinction.

In Magic mode:

```text
legal Pack Rat host operation
-> Magic resolves the card, token, zone, and priority state
-> Deep Hearth records the host result
-> Basin population witness increments
```

In cardless mode:

```text
sample slip
-> 3 Black Reserve
-> sample is archived
-> Rat population token is created
-> organ transcript and State Capsule witness the result
```

The modes are compatible in operation shape, not identical in machinery. A
failed, refused, blocked, or uncertain host operation does not produce an
invented successful result.

## The Hearth REPL

The optional Hearth REPL is the interface layer for a declared organ. It may be a
paper prompt, terminal, Magic surface, calculator, Yupana, or digital tool.

```text
HELP
SHOW
RUN <operation> <input>
WITNESS
REST
```

Before commitment, the organ shows its input, cost, latency, scope, possible
output, and error values. The player may commit or refuse. The organ cannot spend
an unoffered resource, silently change a receiving state, or guess an unavailable
answer.

The REPL transcript is event history, not merely a diagnostic log. It may attach
to a State Capsule and return with the operation later.

## Land Channels

Magic mode uses the host format's actual land and color law. Cardless mode uses a
declared Basin source such as:

- a colored token;
- a marked scenario card;
- a place or altar;
- a faction license;
- a relationship;
- a declared energy tag;
- a physical material.

Native, licensed, learned, and restricted access remain visible in either mode.
An active channel occupies capacity. Breadth brings more channels and more
retuning, switching, maintenance, and setup; depth brings fewer channels and
cheaper, more efficient operations.

### Retuning procedure

At a safe Rest, a character may retune active land channels without an extra
field cost; the new channels and receiving source are witnessed with the Rest.
During active exploration, changing one active slot requires ten uninterrupted
minutes, one Reserve, and a declared receiving source. The change leaves a trace
and can be refused or unavailable if the source cannot receive the request.

This is a Beta candidate. Play should test whether the field cost makes breadth a
meaningful choice without making a character unusable away from Rest.

## Different Textures of Play

Card play can make Deep Hearth feel like an archive, market, spellbook, machine,
or living deck. It supports privacy, prepared possibility, deck-like discovery,
and portable state.

Cardless play can make it feel like a garden, household, route, body, or
conversation. It keeps attention on the world rather than on an external object
and makes improvisation immediate.

Neither mode is more real. The question is what the table should notice.

## Beta Questions

- Which operations become clearer with a card and which become clearer with a
  token, die, or ledger?
- When does a card help preserve memory, and when does it hide state behind a
  collectible-looking object?
- Can a player move an operation from card mode to cardless mode without losing
  its costs, boundaries, or witness?
- Does cardless play remain equally expressive rather than merely available?
- Which host adapters deserve their own physical grammar?
- What should a card carry for a player, GM, Bestiary, or public world cell?
- Which transitions are delightful because a card enters play, and which are
  better when the table creates the material together?

## Completion Observation

This research is ready to promote into beta procedures when a group can run one
operation in card mode and cardless mode, explain what remained invariant, and
describe how the material changed the experience without confusing the rules.
