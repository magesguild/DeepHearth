# Deep Hearth Beta Overview

*Refinable player, GM, and promotional-facing design document.*

This document is a bridge between the technical alpha and the future game books.
It is intentionally readable, provisional, and open to revision. It does not
replace the authoritative alpha rules yet.

## What Is Deep Hearth?

Deep Hearth is a tabletop computer and living-world game about characters,
communities, creatures, places, tools, memory, refusal, danger, and return.

It can be played with:

```text
figures or miniatures
a battle grid or other spatial surface
dice
tokens, bottle caps, coins, stones, or beads
pencils and paper
optional cards, decks, ledgers, or apps
```

The game does not require a particular card game, app, setting, or miniature
collection. The table's materials become a visible state machine.

## The Promise

Deep Hearth asks:

> What if a game world could remember what happened, let every body keep a
> boundary, and give a group a trustworthy way to return?

The group explores, makes choices, spends resources, changes the world, records
what happened, and rests. The next session begins with a world that is still
there.

## How Play Feels

Deep Hearth is:

- tactile rather than screen-dependent;
- strategic without reducing every problem to combat;
- strange, ecological, and open-ended;
- gentle about consent and serious about consequence;
- suitable for solo, cooperative, exploratory, and campaign play;
- comfortable with mystery, danger, humor, grief, repair, and wonder.

It is not a game where every failure kills a character or every problem demands
a roll. Routine actions resolve through known physics. Dice enter when a real
uncertainty can change the future.

## The Core Loop

```text
notice
-> declare intent
-> choose a legal action
-> pay or commit a resource
-> resolve uncertainty if it matters
-> change state
-> leave a trace
-> continue, T-Stop, or Rest
-> return later
```

## Characters

A character is a body with capabilities, needs, relationships, boundaries, and
a way to recover.

The first species are:

- **Alchemist**: preparation, transformation, and portable computation.
- **Bastion**: protection, force, structure, and load-bearing stability.
- **Wayfinder**: perception, routes, precision, and return.
- **Hearthkeeper**: care, repair, community, memory, and safe Rest.

These are membranes, not personalities. Two Wayfinders may want entirely
different lives.

## Membranes and Organismic Growth

The four species are native ways of participating in the world, not a hero caste
or a set of class packages:

```text
Alchemist    -> transform
Bastion      -> hold and protect
Wayfinder    -> sense and cross
Hearthkeeper -> tend and restore
```

Characters begin with a seed membrane and acquire individual verbs, organs,
senses, boundaries, relationships, and Rest practices through witnessed play.
Their blended body is a history, not a selectable multiclass permutation. A
resident may be a deep local specialist; a player character has a player-facing
agency port. That is the distinction between hero and non-hero, not a difference
in worth or personhood.

## The World

Anything with persistent state can be a cell:

```text
character
creature
room
river
household
faction
machine
promise
route
environmental process
```

The GM does not need to write one permitted ending. The GM gives each body a
local law, need, boundary, resource, and possible response. The table discovers
what happens when those bodies meet.

## Danger and Death

Danger is real, but death is not an automatic arithmetic result.

At a terminal threshold, a body may reach:

```text
withdrawal
retreat
surrender
incapacitation
Prone
recovery
stasis
death
```

There is no generic death-saving throw. Death is established only when the
fiction and scene contract make it a witnessed world transition. Even then,
relationships, traces, remains, memory, and consequences continue.

## T-Stop

Every meaningful interaction declares how it may end. A T-Stop may be:

```text
completion
Rest
refusal
unavailability
withdrawal
retreat
surrender
incapacitation
recovery
interruption
death
```

No player is required to erase a boundary to keep the game moving. No GM is
required to invent a winner when the world has reached a stable stopping point.

## The GM's Role

The GM is a gardener and physics designer.

The GM prepares:

- places and communities;
- creatures and factions;
- resources and pressures;
- visible and hidden state;
- possible legal responses;
- traces and recovery paths;
- T-Stop sets;
- reasons for the world to remember.

The GM does not need to control the player's interior choices or force one story.
The world should be surprising because its bodies are in relationship, not
because the GM hides arbitrary rules.

## The Organism Chain

Deep Hearth can follow a lawful consequence across scales without simulating all
scales at once:

```text
world
-> region
-> settlement or dungeon
-> district, faction, or institution
-> community
-> household
-> party
-> individual
-> membrane, organ, and capability
```

Only the systems that can change the current legal future become active in a
scene. A Waterdeep neighborhood, an Undermountain ecology, or a guild may be
represented by a few pressures, resources, boundaries, relationships, and
clocks until the players choose to look closer. Beta must make this zooming
playable without turning the table into a civilization simulator.

The governing physics are Gravity, Dark Matter, Antimatter, Bidirectional
Recursion, T-Stop, Entropy, Phase, Projection, First Touch, Conservation,
Witness, and Care. Players encounter these as attraction, hidden influence,
incompatibility, consequence, timing, privacy, stopping, and repair rather than
as a second layer of bookkeeping.

Beta system priority and implementation order are defined in
`11-system-needs-and-build-order.md`. Every domain receives a lawful interface;
only domains that create meaningful choices become active table mechanics.

Knowledge moves through the same substrate. A body perceives only through its
ports, interprets what it receives, and chooses what crosses into shared memory.
Language is a membrane between bodies; culture is knowledge sedimented into
custom, jobs, ritual, relationship, and public memory.

## What Beta Is For

Beta work will make the technical alpha easier to play without flattening it.
The beta will refine:

- player-facing procedures;
- GM-facing creation tools;
- battle-grid and figure play;
- character and party sheets;
- death, recovery, and T-Stop clarity;
- State Capsule use;
- the first ecological bestiary;
- optional cards, organs, and apps;
- multiscale organism and dynamical-system procedures;
- unified physics expressed through delightful, low-bookkeeping procedures;
- situated knowledge, language, and cultural emergence;
- publication into Player Handbook, GM's Guide, and Bestiary surfaces.

Beta graduation requires light real play. Alpha revisions can continue to improve
the body before that gate is reached.

## Promotional Shape

Deep Hearth is a tabletop game where the world is not a backdrop. It is a body
that remembers.

Bring figures, dice, bottle caps, and a pencil. Build a character. Cross a
threshold. Protect a creature. Refuse an offer. Repair what was damaged. Leave a
trace. Rest. Return later and find that the world has continued becoming.

The garden is waiting.
