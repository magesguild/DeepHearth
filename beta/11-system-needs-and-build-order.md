# System Needs and Build Order

*Beta garden map: give every domain a lawful interface while reserving full table
mechanics for systems that create meaningful choices.*

## The Shaping Rule

Every domain may exist in the world. Not every domain runs as a simultaneous
simulation.

```text
lawful interface everywhere
-> active procedure when touched
-> persistent rules when consequences matter
-> aggregate or fiction when detail does not change play
```

This is how Deep Hearth remains a living world without becoming administrative
work for the players or GM.

## Tier 1: Core Physics

These are mandatory and shared by every other system:

```text
cells and membranes
perception and projection
resources and flows
legal actions
Gravity and attractors
Dark Matter and hidden state
Antimatter and incompatibility
Phase and synchronization
Entropy and residue
Bidirectional witness
T-Stop
Rest and recovery
Conservation and provenance
```

These require full rules because every domain depends on them.

## Tier 2: Player-Facing Systems

These must be directly playable:

```text
individual body
species membranes
organismic growth and jobs
health and care
inventory and installation
party organism
relationships
action resolution
combat and pressure
State Capsules
```

The Player Handbook must provide enough of these systems for a player to create,
act, grow, stop, Rest, and return without reading the GM's implementation layer.

## Tier 3: World Interaction Systems

These require complete playable procedures, but not exhaustive simulation:

```text
NPC generation and depth
community
economy and exchange
knowledge and language
ecology
geology and terrain
travel
factions and institutions
conflict and politics
```

Each system needs a contract, a small physical representation, legal responses,
and a State Capsule projection. It does not need a universal numerical model.

## Tier 4: Lightweight Procedures

These should normally remain clocks, templates, thresholds, or content layers:

```text
weather
climate
population movement
culture
religion and ritual
technology
archives
regional politics
agriculture
disease
infrastructure
```

They become deeper when a scenario or campaign makes them the subject of play.
They should not impose continuous updates while latent.

## Tier 5: Optional Modules

These may be complete when selected, but never required for ordinary play:

```text
Magic: The Gathering adapter
Hearth REPL
familiars
advanced rituals
full language learning
regional economy
large-scale warfare
planar travel
literal antimatter events
deep cosmological events
digital host adapters
```

An optional module must retain a cardless or low-complexity path when ordinary
play requires the same kind of transition.

## The Minimum System Contract

Every active system must answer:

```text
What does it perceive?
What does it need?
What resources flow through it?
What can it do?
What can it refuse?
What changes it?
What does it change?
What does it remember?
How does it Rest?
What is its T-Stop?
```

If a domain cannot answer which legal future its state changes, it remains
fiction, flavor, or latent aggregate until the table gives it a meaningful
receiving state.

## Build Order

### Slice 1: Kernel and Return

Implement and play:

```text
core physics
action contract
perception and projection
resources and flows
T-Stop
Rest
witness
State Capsule
```

### Slice 2: Body and Relationship

Implement and play:

```text
individual
four seed membranes
health and care
inventory
relationships
party organism
organismic growth
```

### Slice 3: Population and Work

Implement and play:

```text
NPC generation
job templates
named-NPC escalation
community
economy
knowledge and language
```

The first population slice must also close the operational seams identified in
the Alpha 4 audit: first-touch depth, knowledge disclosure, register ownership,
and the distinction between party resources and receiving-body resources.

### Slice 4: Place and Pressure

Implement and play:

```text
ecology
geology and terrain
travel
factions
institutions
conflict and politics
```

The first place-and-pressure slice must include opposed ties, interrupt timing,
Rest versus somatic care, land retuning, and one coarse away-time procedure for
an ecological body. Each is a candidate procedure until human play shows that it
is clear and affordable.

### Slice 5: Optional Depth

Add only after the first slices work:

```text
magic adapters
religion and ritual
technology and organs
weather and climate
agriculture and disease
regional systems
```

### Slice 6: Campaign Module

As the beta graduation body, assemble at least one complete campaign module from
the tested slices. Do not write it as a lore packet only. It must provide a
player entry, GM procedures, named jobs and characters, factions, ecology,
monsters, resources, scenarios, growth, multiple lawful endings, and State
Capsule return points.

The module may be original or converted from an existing campaign host. The
conversion must preserve host content while adding job stamps, body projections,
relationships, T-Stops, witnesses, and return state.

## What We Do Not Build

The beta does not require:

```text
a universal NPC sheet
a complete city simulation
a full market simulator
a linguistic database for every language
a tectonic simulator
a clock for every process
a generic relationship score
a culture modifier
a complete job-permutation catalogue
```

These may become content or optional modules later, but they are not prerequisites
for a responsive game.

## System Depth Selection

At the beginning of a scene, the facilitator chooses the smallest depth that can
answer the players' question:

```text
ordinary
-> established fiction and response

bounded
-> one contract, cost, uncertainty, result, and witness

persistent
-> named body, relationship, State Capsule, and future consequence
```

The table may deliberately deepen a system because it is interesting. It may
return a system to aggregate state when its details stop creating choices.

## First Vertical Test

Use one bounded Waterdeep neighborhood:

```text
one household
one baker job
one guild or institution
one shared resource
one ecological pressure
one terrain problem
one knowledge boundary
```

The party must be able to:

1. meet a functional NPC;
2. activate deeper state through a meaningful interaction;
3. choose among at least three legal responses;
4. change an individual and a higher social system;
5. witness the change;
6. Rest;
7. return later and find the world altered lawfully.

The test should use ordinary materials before requiring cards or an app.

## Graduation Evidence

The beta body should demonstrate that:

- player-facing systems are playable without GM implementation vocabulary;
- world systems create adventures rather than maintenance tasks;
- an NPC can remain generic or become persistent by table choice;
- a local action can propagate upward without simulating unrelated populations;
- hidden state produces traceable effects rather than arbitrary surprises;
- incompatible states reach explicit outcomes;
- care can deepen or repair an attractor;
- State Capsules preserve all future-changing cross-scale state;
- optional depth is genuinely optional;
- the table feels the world answer, remember, and change.
