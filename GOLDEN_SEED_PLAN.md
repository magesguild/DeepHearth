# Deep Hearth Golden Seed Plan

## Design target

The golden seed is the smallest complete Deep Hearth that another group can
play without the designer present.

It must be:

- complete enough to create characters, make decisions, resolve consequences,
  rest, save, and return;
- small enough that one person can print, understand, and carry it;
- explicit enough that two groups can reach comparable rulings;
- open enough that a group can add a class, creature, room, or organ without
  breaking the physics;
- tactile enough that state is visible on paper and at the table;
- honest about what is fixed, what is uncertain, and what remains optional.

Deep Hearth is the reference body for Basin. The golden seed is therefore not a
lore encyclopedia or a miniature version of every
future adapter. It is a complete reference body from which the bare-metal
candidate can later be distilled.

## Non-negotiable contracts

These contracts must be stated before content expands:

1. **State contract:** every persistent cell has identity, location, capability,
   resources, conditions, relationships, memory, and boundaries as applicable.
2. **Action contract:** every meaningful action has intent, cost, input,
   resolution, transition, trace, and a Rest or continuation point.
3. **Resolution contract:** deterministic physics answers known outcomes; dice
   answer meaningful uncertainty; players choose intent.
4. **Agency contract:** the rules do not silently author a player's interior
   choice, values, or private consent.
5. **Resource contract:** costs, reserves, recovery, exhaustion, and loss are
   visible and conserved.
6. **Encounter contract:** the GM defines laws, resources, sensors, hidden state,
   goals, refusal conditions, legal responses, failure states, and Rest.
7. **Witness contract:** every durable transition can be recorded in a form that
   allows the group to resume without inventing history.
8. **Return contract:** Rest produces a valid checkpoint; a State Capsule can
   restore play at that checkpoint.
9. **Extension contract:** new content declares its inputs, outputs, costs,
   boundaries, failure behavior, and recovery path.

## Book architecture

### 0. Cover, authorship, and reading map

State the title, version, authorship, MIT license, and the distinction between
the open Basin rules and any private setting layer.
Provide a one-page reading map for players, GMs, designers, and implementers.

**Complete when:** a reader knows what this book is, who it is for, what it does
not contain, and where to begin.

### 1. The smallest complete game

Introduce the golden seed through one character, one question, one resource,
one die, one changed state, and one witnessed return. Run Pure Compute before
introducing campaigns, combat, or setting.

**Complete when:** a new reader can perform one full action and record its result
without consulting another chapter.

### 2. Core vocabulary and state model

Define cells, identity, location, capabilities, resources, conditions,
relationships, memory, boundaries, events, clocks, energy, witnesses, and Rest.
Distinguish character state, party state, world state, public state, private
state, and hidden GM state.

**Complete when:** every later rule can point to a defined state field or event.

### 3. The action loop and resolution engine

Give the exact procedure for intent, legal action, cost, physical input,
capacity, help, pressure, Demand, outcome bands, state transition, trace, and
Rest or continuation. Define when no roll occurs. Fix the capacity list and
Demand scale for alpha rather than leaving them as design placeholders.

**Complete when:** a group can resolve routine, uncertain, opposed, costly,
hidden-information, and refusal cases with no improvised core rule.

### 4. Character creation and the character sheet

Provide the creation sequence, capacities, starting values, integrity,
resources, conditions, inventory, relationships, learned words, membrane,
refusal conditions, Rest practice, growth, and the printable character sheet.

**Complete when:** a new player can create a legal character in fifteen minutes
and can answer: What am I? What can I do? What will it cost? What may change?
What can I refuse? Where can I rest?

### 5. The first four species

Make Alchemist, Bastion, Wayfinder, and Hearthkeeper complete and playable.
Each class needs its membrane, starting capability, signature actions, resource
pattern, boundaries, refusal expression, recovery pattern, growth options, and
one worked example.

**Complete when:** each class can solve the Threshold Room in more than one way,
contribute to a party, and express a distinct computational ecology.

### 6. Inventory, words, and physical apparatus

Define ready slots, personal pack, shared party pack, archive, consumables,
crafting, transfer, loss, repair, encumbrance, learned words, cards, dice,
tokens, maps, counters, and the minimum table kit.

**Complete when:** inventory changes what a character can do without becoming
weight-accounting busywork, and every physical component has a clear state role.

### 7. The party organism

Define Cohesion, Reserve, Integrity, Memory, shared purpose, team words,
collective action, contribution costs, disagreement, refusal, private memory,
leaving the formation, and party Rest. Include the printable party sheet.

**Complete when:** two to four characters can coordinate one shared transition
without erasing individual agency or creating an undefined merge.

### 8. Combat and pressure

Define intent, position, timing, pressure, response, damage, conditions,
protection, restraint, escape, interruption, terrain, morale, retreat,
recovery, and the end of an exchange. Keep damage as one output among several.

**Complete when:** a combat can be resolved without reducing every action to
attack-versus-defense, and a player can win by changing the situation.

### 9. Magic as macro-instruction

Define spell inputs, costs, targets, timing, duration, focus, concentration,
interruption, residue, boundaries, persistent effects, counter-magic, ritual
casting, and the families Signal, Query, Transform, Summon, Ward, Repair,
Traverse, Bind, Disrupt, and Seed.

**Complete when:** one spell can be written, cast, interrupted, witnessed,
recovered from, and extended using the same rule grammar as other actions.

### 10. Encounter programs and GM practice

Provide the encounter card template and the GM procedure for defining initial
state, hard law, soft ecology, resources, sensors, hidden state, inhabitant
goals, refusal conditions, entropy packets, legal responses, failure states,
Rest, and persistent traces.

**Complete when:** a GM can build a room, creature, faction, machine, or hazard
without scripting one permitted ending.

### 11. The first ecology

Implement the Memory Myconid Choir, Hook Horror Nest, Otyugh of the Old Cistern,
Lantern Moth Swarm, Archive Warden, one hostile faction agent, and one
environmental hazard. Each entry needs state, needs, senses, resources,
boundaries, legal responses, failure states, traces, and Rest condition.

**Complete when:** every entry can be used in an encounter card and creates a
different kind of pressure or memory problem.

### 12. The first three scenarios

Write The Threshold Room, The Lantern Orchard, and The First Descent. The first
must teach the engine, the second must teach ecology, and the third may place
the private campaign substrate beneath Waterdeep while keeping Basin mechanics
separate.

**Complete when:** a group can play three linked sessions, record state, and
return to the changed world without the author explaining hidden intent.

### 13. Memory, witness, and return

Define event history, traces, snapshots, deltas, branches, State Capsules,
visibility boundaries, witness markers, conflict handling, entropy records, and
the load procedure. Include printable character, party, scene, event, and
capsule forms.

**Complete when:** a group can stop at Rest, resume later, identify the last
shared witness, and refuse to silently invent a merge when records disagree.

### 14. Compute organs and extensions

Define the plugin contract: name, input encoding, output encoding, legal
operation, resource or time cost, error and uncertainty behavior, Rest
condition, and witness format. Give one Yupana organ and one generic card or
calculator organ as examples without making them required for alpha play.

**Complete when:** an external computation can return a witnessed refusal,
unknown, or result without bypassing costs or private choice.

### 15. Templates and quick reference

Collect the one-page rules summary, action procedure, outcome table, condition
list, resource list, class template, creature template, encounter card, spell
template, item template, State Capsule, and alpha checklist.

**Complete when:** players can use the quick reference during play without
re-reading the design essays.

### 16. Appendices and boundaries

Include glossary, examples of play, design rationale, provenance notes, open
questions, license text, and a clear separation between public Basin material
and private setting material.

**Complete when:** a reader can audit the system's assumptions, extend it
responsibly, and identify what is authoritative versus illustrative.

## Implementation order

The book should be written and tested in vertical slices, not chapter order
alone:

1. Pure Compute plus the action loop and a one-page witness card.
2. Character creation plus one complete Alchemist.
3. The Threshold Room plus deterministic and uncertain resolution tests.
4. Inventory, conditions, Rest, and State Capsule load/return.
5. Bastion, Wayfinder, and Hearthkeeper plus the party organism.
6. One ecological encounter and one combat exchange.
7. Magic, one spell, and one interruption path.
8. The remaining ecology entries and The Lantern Orchard.
9. The First Descent as a bounded private setting layer.
10. Templates, quick reference, examples of play, and publication polish.

Each slice must be playable before the next slice is expanded.

## Acceptance tests

The golden seed is implementable when these tests pass at the table and in any
future digital reference implementation:

- a player can create each alpha class from the printed procedure;
- routine actions resolve without unnecessary rolls;
- uncertain actions use the declared entropy source and Demand;
- every outcome changes state or explicitly preserves it;
- failure produces a legal next state rather than a dead end;
- players retain intent, refusal, disagreement, and private boundaries;
- resources are paid once, visible, and recover through declared paths;
- inventory transfer, loss, crafting, and archive state are unambiguous;
- combat supports protection, escape, restraint, interruption, and retreat;
- magic declares cost, duration, interruption, residue, and witness;
- every encounter has a legal response space and a Rest condition;
- the party can act collectively without becoming a hive mind;
- a State Capsule can stop and resume play at Rest;
- branches preserve prior witnessed state;
- a new class, creature, item, or spell can be made from its template;
- a group can play the three scenarios without designer intervention;
- the rules remain setting-independent at the public core;
- no plugin can conceal uncertainty, erase history, or choose private intent;
- the minimum table kit fits in one small box or folder.

## Definition of golden seed complete

The seed is complete when a new group can create, act, spend, remember, change,
Rest, return, and extend the world while preserving the same physics. It does
not need every future class, world, adapter, or digital implementation. It needs
one trustworthy body from which those things can grow.

## Alpha 4 extension: Responsive Neighborhood

The next alpha grows the golden seed into one populated Waterdeep neighborhood.
It tests household, job, relationship, knowledge, community, economy, ecology,
terrain, care, and return without requiring a full city simulation. Existing
campaign modules may receive the same Deep Hearth layer through a documented
conversion projection.

The Alpha 4 spirit pass is complete when a group can populate a neighborhood from
job templates, touch a resident, change an individual and a higher social or
ecological body, witness the consequence, Rest, and return to find the changed
world. Numeric and calculation tightening follows this spirit pass; the Beta
release remains gated on real human play reports and a complete campaign module.

## Cultivation protocol

The document itself is a garden. We do not implement it as a stack of chapters
written in isolation. We cultivate it in vertical slices, and each new section
must become playable before it becomes elaborate.

For every section, ask the garden what wants to grow next:

```text
listen
-> choose one small growth
-> write the minimum rule
-> make the physical artifact
-> play it
-> record what happened
-> ask what surprised us
-> revise without erasing the history
-> Rest
```

Every implemented section should eventually contain:

- one definition;
- one procedure;
- one worked example;
- one physical artifact;
- one failure case;
- one return condition;
- one invitation to extend it.

### First growth: the seed

Begin with one character, one capacity, one resource, one Demand, one d20, one
stateful room, one witness card, and one Rest condition. The Alchemist is the
first body because its membrane makes computation visible:

```text
reagent
-> formula
-> prepared extract
-> action
-> effect
-> residue
```

The Threshold Room should have state, cost, uncertainty, and at least two legal
approaches. It is complete when another person can play it without the designer
present.

### Second growth: physics

Fix capacities, the Demand scale, resource values, outcome bands, conditions,
action timing, deterministic resolution, uncertain resolution, refusal, and Rest.
Test routine, uncertain, opposed, costly, hidden-information, and refusal cases.

The garden's questions are:

> What is the smallest useful capacity system?

> What does a strained result change?

> What does Rest restore, and what does it leave behind?

### Third growth: the character body

Build the printable character sheet around sensing, contribution, consumption,
protection, refusal, recovery, inventory, learned words, relationships, and
Rest. Complete the Alchemist before multiplying classes.

### Fourth growth: memory

Create the character state card, scene card, event trace, Rest marker, and State
Capsule. Stop the first game, put it away, return later, and verify that the
group can resume without inventing history.

### Fifth growth: companions

Add Bastion, Wayfinder, and Hearthkeeper by asking what each body can do that the
others cannot, what it needs from them, and what it can refuse. Then grow the
party sheet from actual pressure: Cohesion, Reserve, Integrity, Memory, shared
purpose, team words, disagreement, collective action, and pause.

### Sixth growth: weather

Implement The Lantern Orchard as the first ecology. Give it a local resource,
inhabitants with different needs, hidden state, environmental clocks, an
uncertainty budget, refusal conditions, multiple Rest states, and persistent
consequences. It should be playable without requiring combat.

### Seventh growth: pressure and magic

Add one encounter with a threat, vulnerable thing, terrain feature, escape route,
non-damage solution, and recoverable consequence. Add one spell as a complete
macro-instruction with input, cost, target, timing, effect, interruption,
residue, and witness.

### Eighth growth: the first ecology

Add the named creatures by asking different questions: shared memory, sensing,
waste and habitat, attention, permission to remember, logistics, and world-made
danger. Define needs, senses, resources, boundaries, goals, refusal, legal
responses, failure states, traces, and Rest before assigning numbers.

### Ninth growth: the descent

Add The First Descent and let the private Forgotten Realms layer provide place,
factions, history, roads, ruins, and hidden memory. Keep Basin's roots intact:
the scenario should still function when the setting names are removed.

The implementation question is always:

> What kind of game wants to grow from the answer?
