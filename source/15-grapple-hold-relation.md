# Grapple as Hold Relation

## Contact is a relation

Grapple is not a universal condition that switches a character off. It is a
temporary contested membrane between two bodies.

```text
body A
<-> contact relation
body B
```

The relation records:

```text
participants
contact points
position
anchor
control
legal actions
escape routes
resource cost
injury or pressure
release condition
witness
```

The combat clock governs when the relation can change. The relation itself
persists between meaningful transitions.

## The relation card

```text
GRAPPLE / HOLD RELATION

Body A: ______________________
Body B: ______________________
Contact: _____________________
Anchor: ______________________
State: contact / leverage / restraint

A wants: _____________________
B wants: _____________________
A can currently: ______________
B can currently: ______________

Cycle cost: ___________________
Escape routes: ________________
Externalities: ________________
Release condition: _____________
Witness: ______________________
Rest or continuation: __________
```

The card names what is restricted. It never silently claims that a body has no
choice.

## Relation states

### Contact

The bodies are engaged, but neither has decisive control. Movement, equipment,
attention, and available actions may be affected, but both bodies retain a broad
legal action space.

### Leverage

One body has a positional advantage. That body may attempt to move, redirect,
disarm, protect, or improve the hold. The other may escape, reverse, strike,
bargain, call for help, change terrain, yield, or accept a known consequence.

### Restraint

A specific action space is constrained:

```text
cannot leave this square
cannot reach the lantern
cannot use this hand
cannot complete this gesture
cannot cross this boundary
```

Restraint does not determine private values, thoughts, speech, or consent.

## Starting contact

If both bodies accept contact, establish `Contact` without a roll. The bodies
declare what the contact is for and what ends it.

If contact is contested, both bodies declare a move and spend the relevant cycle
cost before the transition is resolved.

Contact may also be established deterministically by the world: a body falls
onto another, a net closes, a door pins an arm, or a creature catches someone at
an established boundary.

## Moves and costs

```text
hold or maintain position       1 cycle
escape or create distance       2 cycles
redirect or move the other      2 cycles
protect a body within contact   2 cycles
strike or disarm                3 cycles
improve to leverage             3 cycles
establish restraint             3 cycles
call, speak, or signal          1 cycle
yield or release                1 cycle
```

Equipment, terrain, assistance, and conditions may change a cost or pressure.
They do not erase the relation's boundaries.

## Contested transitions

When both bodies want incompatible transitions, use one bounded contest.

Each body declares:

```text
intent
capacity
resource cost
help or equipment
acceptable consequence
```

Each body rolls:

```text
d20 + relevant capacity + helpful contribution - pressure
```

The higher total leads the transition. A difference of five or more is a strong
transition. A difference of one to four is an ordinary transition. A tie is a
strained contact: neither body gains decisive control, both consequences remain
visible, and the relation continues at the current state.

The die resolves the contested physical future. It does not decide whether a
body wants to keep struggling, yield, bargain, or stop at the next legal window.

## Legal responses

The body without control is never reduced to “do nothing.” It may choose among
the actions allowed by the relation:

```text
escape
reverse
strike
disarm
protect
call for help
change terrain
bargain
yield
wait
release if able
```

If no action can change the relation this turn, the body may preserve a declared
boundary, spend cycles on preparation, or reach a still point. The table does
not roll repeatedly merely to demonstrate that contact continues.

## Equipment and anchors

Equipment changes the relation through declared operations:

```text
rope       -> extends restraint and creates an anchor
manacles   -> converts leverage into durable restraint
net        -> creates broad contact that can be cut or escaped
shield     -> redirects pressure or protects a body
hook       -> creates positional contact
armor      -> reduces some consequences and may add fatigue
wall       -> anchors position
oil        -> changes traction and escape
ward       -> declares a magical boundary
```

The object does not replace the relation. It gives the relation another input,
cost, boundary, or escape route.

## Violence and still points

Grappling may produce injury, but injury is not the only output. The relation may
end in:

```text
release
escape
restraint
surrender
separation
injury and repair
retreat
incapacitation
death when explicitly established
Rest
```

Yielding is a legal state, not a moral defeat. A depleted body may withdraw,
shelter, surrender, enter stasis, repair, and return. The victor does not acquire
the defeated body's interior will.

## Dice habitat

```text
primary: d20 contested transition
cycle registers: one d6 per body
relation register: d4 contact / leverage / restraint / release window
support: equipment, terrain, and declared pressure
entropy budget: one roll per meaningful contested transition
witness: relation card and event trace
```

The d4 may remain face-up as a relation register. It is not rolled each turn.
The d6 cycle die is turned down as bodies spend time. The d20 enters only when
control meaningfully changes.

## Worked exchange

A cornered boar charges a Wayfinder near a low wall. The Wayfinder spends three
cycles to redirect the charge. The boar spends three cycles to break through.

Both declare their intent. The Wayfinder rolls d20 + relevant capacity and the
boar resolves its charge response with its established pressure. The higher
transition determines whether the relation becomes Contact, Leverage, or a
changed terrain state.

If the Wayfinder wins by two, the boar is redirected into Contact against the
wall, not automatically restrained. If the boar wins by six, it achieves a strong
charge transition and opens the retreat route on its side. If they tie, both
remain in Contact, the wall and charge consequences are recorded, and neither
body is forced to yield.

## Completion test

This slice is complete when a group can:

- establish willing or contested contact;
- represent contact, leverage, and restraint separately;
- spend bounded cycles on grapple actions;
- resolve one contested transition with a d20;
- preserve meaningful legal responses for both bodies;
- use equipment and terrain as relation inputs;
- end the relation through escape, release, surrender, repair, retreat, or Rest;
- reach a deadlock without inventing a winner;
- witness what the relation changed.

Grappling is now a small game of position, time, boundaries, and choice rather
than a binary condition attached to a body.
