# The Threshold Receives

## A return needs an answer

The first return brought evidence from the far side. This slice gives the
Threshold Room a bounded response.

The response is not a voice, prophecy, reward, or secret command. It is a
visible change in the room's public state.

## This minigame

```text
question: how does a place receive its own far-side evidence?
initial state: returned sample, threshold, and visible groove
participants: offering body and threshold physics
moves: offer, keep, withdraw, Rest
apparatus: sample, vial, threshold card, trace marker
uncertainty: none unless the offered material is altered
exit: received, carried, or Rest
T-Stop set: COMPLETE / REFUSED / WITHDRAWN / REST / UNKNOWN
```

The computation is the change in public relation. The threshold does not become
an oracle or a player with hidden motives.

## Dice habitat

```text
primary die: none
support dice: none
persistent registers: reception and reciprocal trace
entropy budget: none for offering known material
witness: visible groove and room card
```

The threshold's answer is physical state, not a random message. A later body may
declare a hidden route question and select a different die habitat.

The player asks:

> What does the threshold do when it receives evidence from its own far side?

## Offer or keep

At the threshold, the player may keep the sealed vial as a portable witness. If
the player does, the room records that evidence returned but was not offered.
There is no further room transition.

If the player offers the dust to the threshold, place a small portion on the
threshold stone or mark its place on the room card. The threshold receives only
what the player offers. The vial, remaining sample, and player inventory retain
their declared state.

Offering is a choice, not a requirement for completing the return.

## The visible answer

The dust settles into an existing shallow groove in the threshold stone. The
groove was always there; the returned material makes its relation legible.

The facilitator marks a line on the Threshold Room card:

```text
far side <-> threshold
```

This is the **Reciprocal Trace**. It does not open a new door, reveal the prior
visitor, identify a destination, or explain the dust's meaning. It records only
that material has now traveled in both directions.

## Receiving state

The threshold's response is a receiving state, not an ownership claim. It
accepts the offered dust, changes the public trace, and leaves the vial, player,
and prior visitor's story outside that acceptance.

```text
received: offered dust only
not received: private motive, unoffered contents, future consent
externality: reciprocal trace becomes visible
refusal: player may keep the vial instead
```

## Room state

After the threshold receives the offered sample, update the public state:

```text
Reception: received
Far-side evidence: offered
Return trace: reciprocal
Invitation: available
Meaning: unknown
Prior visitor: identity unknown
```

The room has answered by becoming more legible, not more obedient.

If the player keeps the vial sealed:

```text
Reception: not offered
Far-side evidence: carried
Return trace: portable
Invitation: unavailable at this room
Meaning: unknown
```

The portable witness remains real. The player may offer it later.

## The invitation

The Reciprocal Trace creates an input port for another body:

```text
trace
-> inspect relation
-> ask where the route continues
-> preserve or alter the return path
-> Rest or continue
```

The invitation is not a summon. No class, character, or player is compelled to
arrive. A later body may be a Wayfinder, Hearthkeeper, Bastion, Alchemist, or a
body the group has not designed yet.

The room does not say who should come. It makes room for someone to notice.

## What the trace does not say

The Reciprocal Trace does not establish:

- that the prior visitor returned;
- that the prior visitor wanted company;
- that the route is now safe;
- that the far side is friendly;
- that the sample has a hidden power;
- that the next body has a destined role.

It is evidence of relation, not an interpretation of motive.

## The witness addendum

Add this to the State Capsule:

```text
Sample offered: yes / no
Reception: received / not offered
Trace: portable / reciprocal
Trace location: __________________
Invitation: available / unavailable
Meaning: unknown
```

The facilitator and player witness the same visible change. Private reflection
may remain private; the public room state does not depend on it.

## Rest

The player may Rest after offering or keeping the sample. Rest records the room's
new state and preserves the invitation without requiring anyone to answer it.

The room can now wait.

## Completion test

This slice is complete when a group can:

- choose whether to offer or keep returned evidence;
- update the room only with what was actually offered;
- represent a reciprocal relation with a physical trace;
- distinguish a visible answer from an invented interpretation;
- make an invitation available without summoning a character;
- preserve the prior visitor's uncertainty;
- Rest with the threshold more legible and no less free.

The next body may now arrive because the room has made a place for attention.
