# The Exchange at the Threshold

## Two bodies, one relation

The Alchemist and Wayfinder now stand beside the Reciprocal Trace. Each has
something the other may use, and neither is required to give it.

```text
Alchemist -> far-side sample and material witness
Wayfinder  -> route record and reciprocal trace
```

This is not a shop transaction. No body has a universal price, and no exchange
is automatically good because something changed hands. The first economic
question is:

> What can two different bodies share without becoming one body?

## This minigame

```text
question: can two bodies share capability without losing identity?
initial state: two bodies, two packets, two boundaries
participants: Alchemist and Wayfinder
moves: offer, accept, transform, archive, refuse, delay, Rest
apparatus: two contract cards, sample, route record, witness ledger
uncertainty: receiving state and externality
exit: reciprocal, asymmetric, refused, delayed, or Rest
```

The simultaneous contracts are the minigame board. The packets and boundaries
are its pieces. The witness ledger is its output memory.

## Exchange packets

An exchange packet may be material, informational, relational, or temporal:

```text
packet: ______________________
source: ______________________
known state: _________________
unknown state: _______________
cost to offer: _______________
boundary: ____________________
```

The packet's source retains its lineage unless the exchange explicitly transfers
ownership or location. Access, knowledge, and custody are different states.

## The two offers

For the first exchange, use these default offers.

### The Alchemist offers sample access

The Alchemist offers the Wayfinder permission to inspect the far-side dust in
the vial.

```text
offer: inspect far-side dust
receiver: Wayfinder
cost: attention and one declared action
transfer: no
boundary: do not remove or reinterpret the sample without permission
```

The Alchemist may instead offer a pinch of dust as a transferred sample, or may
offer nothing.

### The Wayfinder offers a route record

The Wayfinder offers the Alchemist a portable copy of the witnessed route:

```text
origin: far side
current witness: threshold
route: reciprocal
destination: unknown
return: marked
```

Creating the portable copy spends one Route Thread if the Wayfinder has not
already made one. The Wayfinder may offer inspection of the existing trace
instead, or may offer nothing.

## The exchange contract

Each body fills one contract before either body sees the other's response:

```text
body: _________________________
offer: ________________________
receiving state requested: _____
cost accepted: ________________
what remains private: __________
what I will not give: __________
what I will not receive: _______
response if refused: ___________
Rest after exchange: ___________
```

The two contracts may be written on separate cards and revealed together. This
is a small game-theoretic protection: the first speaker does not silently define
the second body's obligations.

## Legal responses

After both offers are visible, each body may choose a legal response.

### Accept

The receiving body accepts the packet in the declared receiving state. The
offerer's cost is paid, and the exchange becomes witnessed.

### Transform

The receiving body accepts a transformed version of the packet. For example, the
Wayfinder may turn sample inspection into a route observation, while preserving
the dust's unknown meaning.

Transformation must be declared before it occurs. It cannot silently create
knowledge the packet did not contain.

### Archive

The receiving body stores the packet or its record without using it immediately.
The archive has a location, owner or custodian, visibility, and recovery path.

### Refuse

The receiving body declines the packet. The source retains its state unless the
offer's declared cost was explicitly paid before refusal. Refusal records a
boundary, not a failure or debt.

### Delay

The receiving body leaves the offer open. The packet remains with its source,
the proposed cost remains unpaid, and the offer enters public Memory as pending.

## The first exchange procedure

1. Confirm that both bodies are present and can act.
2. Identify each packet's source, state, boundary, and unknowns.
3. Write both exchange contracts privately.
4. Reveal the contracts together.
5. Check that each requested receiving state is legal.
6. Each body accepts, transforms, archives, refuses, or delays independently.
7. Pay only the costs of accepted or declared transformations.
8. Update material, information, relationship, and route state.
9. Record the exchange as a witnessed event.
10. Rest, continue, or leave the relation pending.

## Default reciprocal exchange

If both bodies accept the default offers:

```text
Alchemist
-> permits Wayfinder to inspect far-side dust

Wayfinder
-> gives Alchemist a portable route record
```

The Alchemist's sample remains in the vial. The Wayfinder does not acquire the
dust unless transfer was explicitly offered. The Alchemist receives route
knowledge without acquiring the Wayfinder's private interpretation or future
labor.

The public state becomes:

```text
sample access: granted
sample custody: Alchemist
route record: shared
destination: unknown
relationship: reciprocal exchange witnessed
open obligation: none
```

The bodies have become more capable together without becoming one body.

## Asymmetry and refusal

The offers do not need to match.

The Alchemist may accept the route record and refuse sample access. The
Wayfinder may accept inspection and refuse to create a portable copy. The table
records the asymmetry without forcing compensation.

If both refuse, the exchange still produces knowledge:

```text
offer witnessed
boundary preserved
no packet transferred
relationship remains possible
```

A body may refuse because the receiving state is unsafe, because the cost is too
high, because the packet is not wanted, or because it is not ready. The rules do
not require a body to justify its boundary in order for the refusal to be valid.

## Externalities

Every accepted exchange may change more than the two participants:

```text
party Memory changes
route becomes easier to find
another body may act on the shared record
the threshold's witness grows
future secrecy may decrease
```

The facilitator records externalities without treating them as punishment. A
shared fact can be useful and dangerous at the same time.

## Completion test

This slice is complete when a group can:

- exchange material access and information without using money;
- declare offers, receiving states, costs, boundaries, and refusals;
- reveal simultaneous contracts;
- accept, transform, archive, refuse, or delay;
- preserve lineage and custody separately;
- record asymmetry without inventing debt;
- witness externalities and changed future actions;
- Rest with a reciprocal relation, a refusal, or a pending offer.

The exchange teaches the first economic law of the garden:

> Shared capability does not require shared identity.
