# The Shape of Return

## What we can know

We cannot know the prior visitor's private motive. The room does not authorize us
to invent one.

We can read the material evidence they left:

```text
the Return Mark points toward the threshold
the vial is empty and made to carry
the crossing was witnessed for someone after them
```

From this, the table may state a public conclusion:

> The prior visitor intended a reciprocal, carrying, witnessed return.

This describes the shape of the act, not the whole reason for it. It does not
tell us whom they meant to meet, what they hoped to carry, or whether they
completed the journey.

## Three parts of the shape

### Reciprocal

The path was marked in both directions. The far side was not treated as a place
that only receives visitors. A return was part of the route's design.

### Carrying

The empty vial shows that the visitor expected to bring something back. The
object may have been a sample, a message, a memory, a seed, or something the
far side had not yet named.

### Witnessed

The Return Mark was left where another body could find it. The visitor did not
leave only a private memory. They left enough public evidence for a later body
to understand that a return had been intended.

## The return gesture

The current player may echo this shape without claiming to finish the prior
visitor's story.

### 1. Choose a sample

Choose one small, non-unique thing from the far side that the player can legally
carry. It may be:

- a pinch of dust;
- a drop of water;
- a fallen seed;
- a fragment of harmless material;
- a written observation;
- an unspent word learned from the room.

The player does not take a living being, a private memory, or an object whose
boundary has not been offered.

### 2. Name its public state

Record:

```text
sample: ______________________
found at: ____________________
condition: ___________________
meaning known: yes / no
```

The player may carry something without knowing what it means. Unknown is a
valid state.

### 3. Declare the return

The player declares:

```text
carrier: _____________________
container: empty vial / other
destination: _________________
recipient: ___________________
witness: _____________________
```

The recipient may be a person, a party, the room, a future investigator, or no
one yet. A blank recipient is allowed when the honest answer is unknown.

This declaration is a public exchange contract, not a promise that another body
must accept. It names the offer, the receiving state being requested, the cost
of carrying it, and the consequence if the route changes.

```text
offer: ________________________
receiving state requested: _____
cost: _________________________
externality: __________________
refusal remains legal: yes
```

### 4. Cross back

Use the Crossing Contract. A safe route is deterministic. A Flickering route
preserves its known consequence. The player may refuse to carry the sample back,
leave it on the far side, or change the declaration before crossing.

No roll is added merely because the act has become meaningful.

### 5. Witness the return

At the original threshold, record one of these states:

```text
Return echoed: sample arrived
Return altered: sample arrived changed
Return refused: sample remained on far side
Return unknown: carrier did not complete the route
```

The current player has not proven what happened to the prior visitor. They have
made a new witnessed event in the same ecology.

## The vial as memory

The vial is now an inventory cell:

```text
identity: empty vial
location: far side / carried / threshold
contents: empty / sample / unknown
lineage: prior visitor's vessel
boundary: do not claim the absent visitor's story
memory: return shape witnessed
```

When the vial carries a sample, the sample's identity and condition belong in
the State Capsule. The vial does not become a magic container. It becomes a
portable memory relation between two places.

## Open-hearted limits

The table should not turn the prior visitor into an instruction machine. We do
not decide that:

- the visitor wanted to be rescued;
- the visitor wanted the player to continue their work;
- the visitor was good or dangerous;
- the visitor's return failed;
- the player is chosen to complete it.

The evidence supports a shape. The living players decide whether to echo,
decline, or transform that shape.

## Completion test

This slice is complete when a group can:

- distinguish material evidence from private motive;
- describe a return as reciprocal, carrying, and witnessed;
- choose a bounded sample without stealing an unoffered thing;
- carry an unknown object without inventing its meaning;
- declare a destination, recipient, and witness;
- return or refuse without an unnecessary roll;
- record an echoed, altered, refused, or unknown return;
- preserve the prior visitor's dignity and uncertainty.

The far side has not given us a command. It has given us a way to answer care
with care.
