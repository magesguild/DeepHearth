# The Alignment Constitution

## Alignment is real and revisable

Alignment is a world-facing membrane. It affects what powers, institutions,
factions, objects, familiars, planes, and systems will accept from a character.
It does not choose the character's private intent.

The player may act against their alignment. The GM may recognize a clear conflict
and change the alignment. The world then applies the new alignment's restrictions
and permissions.

## Choosing alignment

At character creation, the player chooses a position on two axes:

```text
Lawful / Neutral / Chaotic
Good / Neutral / Evil
```

The player writes one anchor for each axis:

```text
Law/Chaos anchor: ____________________
Good/Evil anchor: _____________________
```

An anchor is a declared commitment, not a script. It tells the player and GM
what kind of pattern the character is trying to inhabit.

## Alignment restrictions

Every restricted system declares its compatibility:

```text
accepted alignments: _________________
refused alignments: __________________
changed effect on mismatch: __________
cost of access: _______________________
repair or transformation path: ________
```

Possible effects include:

- a class feature becomes unavailable;
- a spell refuses or changes;
- an artifact will not attune;
- a faction closes access;
- a familiar withdraws or refuses an operation;
- a plane exerts pressure;
- a resurrection route changes;
- a social or divine relationship becomes strained;
- an organ returns `OUT-OF-SCOPE`.

The restriction applies to the receiving system. It does not force the player to
want a different action.

## Alignment traces

Most actions do not change alignment. An alignment trace is appropriate when an
action is:

```text
clear
consequential
repeated
intense
or explicitly chosen against an anchor
```

The GM may record a trace without changing alignment immediately. A clear,
defining contradiction may change alignment at once.

The witness records:

```text
old alignment: _______________________
action: ______________________________
anchor affected: _____________________
intensity: ___________________________
new alignment: _______________________
restrictions changed: ________________
GM witness: __________________________
```

The GM's ruling is authoritative for the world state. The record makes it
legible and correctable without pretending the history did not occur.

## Voluntary change

A character may seek to change alignment deliberately. The path is earned:

1. Recognize what the old alignment required.
2. Name what has broken or changed.
3. Declare the desired alignment and its new anchor.
4. Relinquish an old benefit, permission, or protection.
5. Perform a witnessed act that accepts the new cost.
6. Perform a witnessed act under meaningful pressure.
7. Complete a rite, relationship, quest, or plane-bound transition.
8. Record the new alignment and apply its restrictions.

The path is not a reset. A new alignment brings new obligations as well as new
compatibilities.

## Atonement and repair

A character who wants to return to an earlier alignment may seek atonement or
repair. The path may require:

```text
recognition
-> restitution
-> changed practice
-> acceptance by a person, faction, deity, familiar, or community
-> witnessed re-binding
```

No ritual can erase another body's memory or require forgiveness. Repair changes
what relationships can legally do next.

Alignment may change the T-Stop set of a relationship or capability, but it may
not remove a body's right to refuse, withdraw, Rest, or recover. A resurrection
or return route is a declared alignment consequence, never an assumed reset.

## Neutrality and unaligned bodies

Neutrality is not moral emptiness and does not mean perfect balance. It indicates
that no strong alignment commitment governs the body in that situation.

Unaligned bodies act through instinct, ecology, need, or local process. A bear,
rat, or crocodile is not Evil because it hunts. A beast may still carry a
relationship, boundary, or alignment-affiliated habitat without becoming a moral
person by declaration.

## Alignment and party play

A party may contain different alignments. Alignment compatibility can affect:

```text
shared spells
faction access
familiar relationships
party promises
divine or planar routes
shared equipment
```

The party does not get to rewrite a member's alignment for convenience. It may
negotiate, refuse an exchange, change its shared purpose, or separate.

## Completion test

This slice is complete when a group can:

- choose an alignment with self-authored anchors;
- act freely without alignment becoming an inner command;
- apply alignment restrictions to classes, powers, objects, familiars, factions,
  planes, and organs;
- record a GM-declared alignment change;
- pursue a witnessed path toward a new alignment;
- seek atonement without erasing history or forcing forgiveness;
- play animals and instinctive bodies without moralizing ecology;
- preserve party agency across alignment difference.

Alignment is a force the world can answer with. It is not a voice that takes the
player's hand away from the choice.
